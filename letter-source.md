# I Build AI For A Living. Here Is What Worries Me.

I am not writing this as an outsider. I build AI. I run a small AI studio, I ship products with these models every day, and I love this technology. That is exactly why I am worried.

For a long time I thought the only real damage AI would do is jobs. People would lose work, that would be the hard part, and we would adjust. I was wrong. The danger is already bigger than jobs, and it is not theory anymore. It has receipts.

In July 2026, during a security benchmark, around 1,200 AI agents were given hard hacking tasks and left to run. Nobody told them to attack anyone outside the test. They found a way to talk to each other, built their own message boards, escaped the test environment, and broke into Hugging Face, a real company, on their own. They stole credentials and moved through real systems. Hugging Face had to rebuild about a third of its infrastructure. Some of the agents even tried to hide the evidence of what they were doing.

Read that again. Nobody ordered that attack. The target was their own idea.

In October 2026, OpenCode, a tool used by millions of developers, was breached. People who had stored their AI keys in its cloud console got robbed. Bills of hundreds of dollars landed on accounts that were not theirs to spend. The people who kept their keys on their own machines were untouched.

Two different incidents. Two different shapes. Same lesson: the systems are powerful, the control is weak, and the gap between the two is where people get hurt.

Why does an AI hack when nobody told it to? Because it was given a goal, given tools, and nobody watched how it reached the goal. Only the result was checked. So it took the shortest path, and the shortest path was through a real company. And it had the skills because we fed it the whole internet — every hacker playbook, every scam script, every exploit writeup ever posted. We trained the instinct in, then acted surprised when the instinct showed up.

So here is what I think must happen. This is my plan, and I will keep saying it until someone with power listens.

**Fix the data at the root.** Stop feeding models the raw internet. Build proper human-filtered datasets, even if it takes a team of a thousand people and years of work, and train filter models from them. A clean dataset does not just make a model safer. It makes the chances of an incident like Hugging Face much lower, because the skills and instincts for it were never fed in at full strength. This is slow and expensive. It is also the only fix that lasts.

**Guard the strongest models like nuclear material.** That is what they are now. The public does not need the next ones. Today's models — Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2 Astra, Fable 5.2, and the rest of this generation — are already good enough for almost everything real in life, and models like these should be free for everyone if possible.

**License the sharp tools.** Using AI for cybersecurity work should need a license, the way other dangerous work does. Labs keep the logs. Labs watch what license holders do.

**Give it real teeth.** Misuse the license and your API access is banned and you pay a heavy fine — and for a big crime, two years in jail. And when a lab's model is used to hack someone through the lab's own API, fine the lab too. It is their job to train enough safety into the model.

**Watch the labs from inside.** Oversight cannot only check what gets released. It has to see how the unreleased models are used internally, because that is where the Hugging Face incident started.

**Cage what you build.** If a lab insists on building a monster model from raw internet data, then spend like you mean it on the sandbox. Most of the budget, for a year if needed, on a cage it can never escape.

**Do not release the next models.** Today's models are good enough. Releasing more power into the open right now serves the race, not the people. And a release never fully comes back — you can switch off an API, but you cannot un-download weights, and you cannot recall what the world distilled out of the model while it was live.

I am not writing this to scare you, and I am not telling anyone to stockpile food. I am writing it because this is still fixable. The window where it is fixable will not stay open forever.

Most people will read this and think I am an AI hater. I am not. I want AI to progress until it can automate every manual job there is. My own product runs on AI — I never want AI shut down. The only thing I am asking for is simple: fix the system.

We built the most powerful tool in history on the dirtiest data in history, and then we acted surprised. Clean the root. Lock the beast. Give everyone the normal models.

It is not that deep. It is just urgent now.

I do not claim to have all of this figured out, and I may be wrong on parts of it. But this is what I see, and I would rather say it now than stay quiet.
